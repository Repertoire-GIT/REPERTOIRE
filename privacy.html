Repertoire privacy policy

Effective 26 November 2026.

Repertoire is a workout tracker that works entirely on your phone. It has no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. The developer can't see your workouts or anything else you put in the app.

This policy explains what the app keeps on your phone, and the few ways information can leave your phone, always because you chose to send it.

Who we are: Repertoire is made by Daninjo Holband, an independent developer. Contact: repsup1@outlook.com

1. What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is stored only on your device, in the app's own storage:

  • workouts, sets, exercises (including ones you create), routines and training plans
  • body weight entries and height, if you enter them
  • your training profile: goal, sex (if you choose to give it), experience, days per week, session length, your gym's equipment, and any notes you write (for example injuries to avoid)
  • friends' progress you've added (see section 5)
  • settings such as units, reminders and rest times

Ranks, records, grades and statistics are calculated on your phone from your logged sets; they are not sent anywhere. Reminders and rest-timer alerts are scheduled on your phone; no push service is used.

The app reads your clipboard only when you tap a "Paste" button.

Deleting your data: you can delete workouts and other items in the app. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your phone. The developer holds no copy, so there is nothing else to delete.

2. Backups you export

"Export all data" creates a file with your data and opens your phone's share sheet. Where that file goes (Files, iCloud Drive, email, a computer) is your choice, and the service you pick handles it under its own terms. The app never uploads backups by itself.

3. Emails you send us (suggestions and feedback)

Three places in the app open your own mail app with a draft addressed to repsup1@outlook.com: "Contact and feedback" in Settings, "Suggest adding this to the app" on exercises you created, and "Suggest it to us" when a search finds nothing.

  • What we receive: only what you choose to send: your email address, your message, and what the draft fills in for you: the exercise details you entered (name, muscles, how-to, notes) or the word you searched for, plus the app's name, version and your phone's platform (iOS or Android). You can read and change the draft before sending, or not send it at all.
  • How it's sent: by your own mail app, not by Repertoire. The mailbox is hosted by Microsoft Outlook.
  • What we use it for: to reply to you and to improve the app (for example, adding a suggested exercise). We don't use it for marketing, don't sell or share it, and don't combine it with anything else, because we have nothing else about you.
  • How long we keep it: as long as needed to handle your message, and no longer than 12 months; sooner if you ask us to delete it.

4. AI plan prompts (copied by you into a chatbot)

"Get a plan" and "Copy my progress" build a text prompt and copy it to your clipboard, so you can paste it into an AI chatbot of your choice, such as ChatGPT. The app does not send this prompt anywhere itself, and the developer never sees it.

  • What a prompt can contain: your goal, sex (if given), experience, training days per week, session length, the notes you wrote in your training profile (current build, injuries or exercises to avoid, other notes), and the exercises available at your gym. "Copy my progress" also includes your current plan, the working sets you logged in the last 4 weeks, recent records, and how many sessions you trained or missed per week.
  • You can see all of it first: "Show prompt" displays the full text before you copy it. Leave out anything you'd rather not share, for example by editing your profile notes.
  • The chatbot you paste into is a third-party service with its own privacy policy, which applies to whatever you send it. The app can open the ChatGPT app or website for you when you ask, but it doesn't log in, send or receive anything there.
  • When you paste the chatbot's reply back, it is read on your phone only.

5. Share cards and friend progress codes (sent by you)

These leave your phone only when you send them yourself, through your phone's share sheet, to the people or apps you choose. The developer doesn't receive them.

  • Workout share card (an image): the workout's date, number of exercises, weight lifted, duration, your grade, any ranks reached, a body map of the muscles trained, and the app's name.
  • Progress code ("Share my progress"): the display name you type, your rank and best set for each main lift, overall rank, number of workouts in the last 4 weeks, weekly-goal streak, lifetime weight lifted, the date, and a random code that lets your friends' apps recognise an updated snapshot from you. It never contains your body weight, weigh-ins or workout history. The app shows exactly what's in it before you share.
  • Friends' codes you add are stored only on your phone, are included in backups you export, and can be removed at any time.

6. Beta testing through TestFlight

If you test Repertoire through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports and feedback you choose to submit with the developer, as described in Apple's TestFlight terms. That is handled by Apple, not by the app.

7. Children

Repertoire is not directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly receive information from them.

8. Your rights

Because the app keeps your data on your phone, you're in control of it: you can view, export and delete it at any time. For emails you've sent us, you can ask us at repsup1@outlook.com to see, correct or delete them, and you can complain to your local data protection authority.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at the same address with a new effective date. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the app's release notes.
